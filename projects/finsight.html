FINSIGHT

Bank statement in, plain-language spending picture out. FINSIGHT reads a South African bank statement PDF and returns a picture of where the money actually went — classified, broken down by day, and written for someone who has never opened a spreadsheet.

The problem

Everyone with a bank account already has a detailed record of their spending, and almost nobody can read it. A statement is a flat list of cryptic merchant strings and reference codes, ordered by date, with no notion of what a transaction was for.

The goal was to close that gap without asking anything of the user: upload the statement, get an answer.

What I built

pdfplumber parses the statement into structured transactions, feature engineering turns each merchant descriptor into model input, and a trained XGBoost classifier sorts it into a spending category — groceries, transport, utilities, money sent and the rest. South African descriptors are messy enough that rules alone don't hold up: VISA DDA PUR 449215 FNB EWALLET MAT 1036 * GP is not obviously a transfer. The extractor uses one method for every statement layout rather than bank-specific parsing code.

The Gradio front end serves the result as Plotly charts: daily spend stacked by category, the largest single purchases ranked by what they took out of the month, and a budget-versus-reality view against a plan the user sets. Each prediction carries the model's confidence, so a wrong guess can be spotted instead of quietly believed.

Result

FINSIGHT is live. A user uploads a statement and gets a categorised breakdown back without configuring anything or naming a single merchant.

  • Classification confidence surfaced to the user, not hidden behind a single label
  • Accounts, saved budgets and analysis history persisted in SQLite
  • One extraction method covers every statement layout — no bank-specific parsing code
